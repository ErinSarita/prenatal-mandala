The Pregnancy and Birth Mandala

A teaching map of pregnancy and birth, read from the center out. The child is at the heart. Around her is the mother. Around the mother is the educator, who brings up possible imprints and best practices for each phase. On the outer petals are practices the mother can use to connect with her baby.

Enter an expected birth date, or the first day of the last period, to see the full moons of this pregnancy and where today falls on the wheel.

Due dates follow Naegele's rule: the first day of the last period plus 280 days (40 weeks). It is an estimate. Labor and birth can come any time from about 37 to 42 weeks, shown on the wheel as the birth window.

About this mandala

A mandala is a circle organized around a center. Traditions across the world have used it as a map: of the cosmos, of the seasons, of the stages of a life. This one maps the roughly forty weeks of pregnancy and the passage of birth through the lens of pre- and perinatal education, where the baby is understood as aware and taking in her world from the very beginning.

How it is built

The circle begins at the top with the first day of the last period, where pregnancy dating starts, and moves clockwise. The first three quarters are the three trimesters, each divided into three months. The final quarter is birth: labor, the birth itself, and the first hour. The wheel closes where it began, at the top, so the end of the journey meets its beginning.

The child, mother, and educator rings move together month by month, so each month of the baby's development lines up with what the mother is going through and how the educator can support the family at that time. The practices on the outer petals are gathered by trimester. Reading straight outward from any petal shows what is happening to the child, what the mother is going through, what the educator can bring to the family, and how the mother can connect with her baby, all at the same moment. Each ring curves outward like a petal and nests into the ring inside it. Hover over or tap any petal for a short summary, and choose it to read in full.

Around the petals runs the time wheel, which holds every marker of time in the pregnancy. Ticks along its edge count the forty weeks, with a number every fifth week, and gold marks show where each trimester begins and where birth begins. Tap any week to see its dates. Once a date is entered, it marks the last period and the due date, the full and new moons, and the birth window from 37 to 42 weeks, a reminder that the due date is the middle of a range rather than a single day. The trimester names sit just outside.

Around the very edge runs a sacred braid of three strands, in the colors of the child, the mother, and the educator who brings knowledge and helps the mother. It is a symbol of the relationships among the three, woven together, holding the mandala in place.

The ten moons

Many traditions count a pregnancy as ten moons, and the forty weeks of pregnancy do span nine or ten full moons. Entering a date marks the real full and new moons of this pregnancy around the wheel, a fun way to follow the weeks of pregnancy alongside the moon's own cycle of about 29.5 days. The full moons are numbered, the new moons are the small dark circles between them, and any moon in the two weeks after the due date has a dashed outline. Tap or hover over a moon to see its date and how many weeks along the pregnancy is.

A note on language

The lines on what the baby may be sensing, and the possible imprints, are written with care. They draw on research and on the experience of the field's pioneers, and they describe possibilities. Every baby and every pregnancy is its own. Following Karen Strange, the baby is called she.
